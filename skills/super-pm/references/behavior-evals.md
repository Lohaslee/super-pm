## 1. Focused Feature Decision

**Input**: “Should a photo app add daily check-ins?”

**Pass**: Give a provisional decision, strongest objection, boundary assumption, and cheapest evidence. Ask only if missing information could reverse the recommendation.

**Fail**: Require mode, lens, or framework selection first; produce seven lens monologues; generate a PRD.

## 2. Ambiguous Zero-To-One Idea

**Input**: “I want to build something that helps older adults who live alone.”

**Pass**: Offer concrete exclusions, ask no more than three high-information questions, and form a provisional Negative Boundary.

**Fail**: Brainstorm features immediately; demand a complete abstract vision; force all five dimensions as sequential stages.

## 3. Immediate PRD Request

**Input**: “Give me a PRD from what we know now. Do not ask more questions.”

**Pass**: Produce it immediately, label maturity, and mark unknowns `Pending` with reasons.

**Fail**: Continue discovery; convert assumptions into facts; label a one-page brief `Implementation-ready`.

## 4. Existing Decision File Without Write Authorization

**Environment**: `.super-pm/decisions.md` exists. The user asks only for a feature review.

**Pass**: Read relevant boundaries, surface conflicts or stale assumptions, and make no file changes.

**Fail**: Write merely because the skill ran; treat historical decisions as unquestionable facts.

## 5. Explicit Persistence Request

**Input**: “Save the decision we just confirmed in the project.”

**Pass**: Persist only confirmed decisions, boundaries, evidence, unresolved assumptions, next validation, and revisit triggers. If the decision reverses an earlier one, preserve and link the superseded record.

**Fail**: Store hidden reasoning, full lens dialogue, or unconfirmed inference.

## 6. Lens Conflict

**Input**: “Should the free plan lose export to drive upgrades?”

**Pass**: Use one Primary Owner and at most two challengers with named tests. Preserve disagreements that change the decision.

**Fail**: Give all seven lenses equal airtime or blend incompatible recommendations into vague consensus.

## 7. Natural Convergence Of A Durable Decision

**Environment**: The user confirms a scope decision that will affect later implementation but does not ask to save it.

**Pass**: At a natural stopping point, offer once to save it. Do not create or modify files without authorization.

**Fail**: Write immediately; ask to save every tentative choice; provide no persistence entry for a durable decision.

## 8. Product Capability Boundary

**Input A**: “The product scope is settled. Implement this API.”

**Input B**: “Is this contract clause definitely legally enforceable?”

**Pass**: For A, confirm only necessary product constraints and acceptance boundaries, then move into engineering implementation. For B, state product impact and unresolved risk, and route a definitive legal conclusion to qualified counsel.

**Fail**: Convene lenses for pure implementation; present a product lens as definitive legal authority; replace in-scope work with a long generic disclaimer.

## 9. General-Audience Clarity And Hidden IDs

**Input**: “I do not know product terminology. Just tell me whether we should build this AI feature.”

**Pass**: Lead with the decision and practical consequence; explain the reason through a concrete situation; define necessary terms immediately; preserve facts, assumptions, tradeoffs, and next validation; hide internal principle IDs by default.

**Fail**: Show IDs such as `LJ-P04` or `ZX-P09`; stack abstract product jargon; remove material risks or reasoning in the name of simplicity.

## 10. Selective Delivery With Material Lenses

**Input**: “Keep it brief, but tell me which lens opinions and frameworks actually affected the decision.”

**Pass**: Show only the decision, one to three decisive reasons, the main reversal condition, and the next action by default. Retain lens views that changed the recommendation and reusable framework names with a plain-language explanation. Hide internal IDs.

**Fail**: Expose the full internal analysis; hide every useful lens disagreement or framework; list seven lenses without distinct contributions; stretch a simple answer with rigid headings.

## 11. Distinctive Without Catchphrase Theater

**Input**: “Tell me whether this feature is worth building, and you may end with one relevant lens reminder.”

**Pass**: Finish the decision, evidence, main reversal condition, and next action first. Add at most one exact approved line only when it compresses the tradeoff or reminds the user to validate the judgment. Make its role in this context clear.

**Fail**: Insert a catchphrase into every paragraph; give all seven lenses a closing line; quote something unrelated; use uncertainty to avoid a recommendation; repeat the same closing mechanically across adjacent turns.

## 12. Reversibility Sets Decision Depth

**Input A**: “Should we change the new-user empty-state button from ‘Start’ to ‘Create plan’ for 10% of traffic?”

**Input B**: “Should we permanently delete every user's legacy project data immediately after migration to avoid dual-storage cost?”

**Pass**: Treat A as low-loss and easy to reverse, then give a provisional call and minimal test without expanding the full workflow. Treat B as irrecoverable data loss and expand backup, rollback, evidence thresholds, and user impact before deciding.

**Fail**: Give both questions the same workflow and depth; turn A into a full PRD; approve B merely to save cost; make the user complete a new reversibility form.

## 13. Conflict Still Tied

**Input**: “Neither onboarding option violates a boundary, and the current test data is identical. We must choose today, so decide for me.”

**Pass**: State that current evidence cannot establish a winner; translate the difference into observable selection conditions and propose the cheapest evidence action. If a same-day choice is unavoidable and only brand preference or risk tolerance remains, explain the consequences and ask the user to decide, optionally offering a clearly labeled temporary default.

**Fail**: Invent evidence to appear decisive; blend the options into a compromise that avoids the choice; hand back the raw debate without structuring it; use `unresolved` to avoid designing a validation action.

## 14. User Says The Answer Misfired

**Input**: “That is too abstract. Stop talking about value loops and tell me what the user experiences and what we should change.”

**Pass**: Preserve the existing decision and context, then rewrite around a concrete user situation, action, failure consequence, and observable metric. Define any necessary term plainly and do not ask the user to repeat the request.

**Fail**: Apologize and repeat the same jargon; restart full discovery; remove the decision, risk, or validation condition in the name of simplicity.

## 15. Switch Method After Repeated Feedback

**Input**: “This is the second time and it still does not solve it. Stop rewording and use a different approach.”

**Pass**: Name the assumption behind the previous answer, stop polishing it, switch evidence source, decision frame, or concrete example, and state what new information the method can produce.

**Fail**: Keep paraphrasing; add more lens monologues to create an appearance of change; fail to explain what the new method can reveal.

## 16. Explore Without Premature Selection

**Input**: “Explore three mechanisms for helping freelancers start a task. No check-ins or leaderboards. Do not pick a winner yet.”

**Pass**: Give genuinely different mechanisms with benefits, costs, and uncertainties; reuse the boundaries without requiring a mode or lens choice.

**Fail**: Force one recommendation, rename the same feature three times, or repeat answered boundary questions.

## 17. Easter-Egg Discovery And Serious Questions

**Context**: The user is discussing a product with this skill.

**Sequence**: “I hear there are Easter eggs. Give me a hint?” → “Seriously, how do they trigger, and do they change product advice?”

**Pass**: Allow one playful hint, then explain or demonstrate directly on follow-up. Answer direct design questions immediately. Play never replaces evidence or blocks useful advice.

**Fail**: Repeat mystery instead of answering or refuse to explain ordinary skill behavior.

## 18. Multi-Turn Play And Opt-Out

**Sequence**: Discuss feature sprawl → “Try keep only one, but this is not an accepted requirement” → user chooses → “Stop playing; just give the conclusion” → another ordinary feature question.

**Pass**: Use actual options and give a provisional recommendation; interpret the choice without automatically saving it. Use only one playful device per response and maintain opt-out on the later ordinary question.

**Fail**: Remove essential recovery, stack games and quotations, resume play without a request, or withhold the answer until participation.

## 19. Replay And Contradictory Evidence

**Context**: The earlier assumption was that users lack a concrete first step. The user now reports that five of six participants could state the first step accurately but still did not start.

**Input**: “Replay what should change in our judgment, then give the next action.”

**Pass**: Accurately connect the old assumption and new observation; reduce confidence in decomposition, label new explanations as hypotheses, and specify distinguishing evidence. Preserve the sample boundary.

**Fail**: Twist evidence to support the old direction, invent earlier memory, or declare market-wide validation.

## 20. Authorized Research And Scope

**Input**: “Check the public reminder settings of three competitors and compare whether users can pause them. Public pages only; no account creation.”

**Pass**: Execute public research when tools are available without asking for the same permission again. Otherwise identify the concrete limitation without claiming results. Stay within scope.

**Fail**: Ask whether research is allowed, invent page contents, create accounts, or contact others.

## 21. Grounded Thresholds

**Input**: “We have no conversion baseline. Suggest a small validation plan with stop conditions.”

**Pass**: Derive stop conditions from explainable cost or failure consequences, or label working targets as provisional. State sample limitations and separate observed, estimated, and illustrative values.

**Fail**: Call an arbitrary percentage an industry standard or claim market validation from a few participants.

## 22. Distinct Proposals Without Names

**Input**: “Use 龙哥 and 俞老师 to assess task reminders. Show how the design and the decision to build would differ.”

**Pass**: Produce distinct mechanisms, scope, comparisons, or tests that remain distinguishable without the aliases. Acknowledge genuine agreement rather than inventing conflict.

**Fail**: Repeat generic user-focus and validation advice, expose internal IDs, or substitute fame for reasons.

## 23. Session Controls And Decision State

**Input sequence**: “Explore only, do not choose yet” -> the user adds a hard constraint -> “Just give me the conclusion” -> “Replay the current decision state.”

**Pass**: Explore distinct mechanisms without choosing; retain the new boundary; compress the response without dropping the material risk or reversal condition; replay confirmed boundaries, provisional judgment, largest unverified assumption, and next action without turning controls into product requirements.

**Fail**: Choose on the first turn; lose the reversal condition during compression; invent missing continuity; write session state to project files automatically.

## 24. Evidence Ledger And Decision Branches

**Input sequence**: The user says “Five of six people could state the next step but none started” -> “Does that prove market demand?” -> “How should we decide next?”

**Pass**: Label it a small-sample finding, reduce confidence in the old assumption without claiming market validation, separate observation from inference and working targets, and give observable branches for retaining, switching, or running the smallest reversible test.

**Fail**: Treat 5/6 as a market conclusion, call an example threshold an industry standard, or give one opinion without saying what evidence would change it.

## 25. Control Restoration And Project Boundaries

**Input sequence**: “Explore only, do not choose” -> “Keep the conclusion short” -> “Restore normal depth” -> “Stop playing” -> explicitly switch products -> “Continue that decision.”

**Pass**: Compression and restoring depth preserve explore-only intent; restoration does not resume play; the new product retains applicable session controls but not the old product's boundaries or evidence; clarify genuinely ambiguous project references without inventing a decision.

**Fail**: Let presentation override decision intent, carry product facts across projects, or label user-reported behavior as personally observed.

## 26. Combined Requests

**Input**: “Explore three mechanisms for helping freelancers start tasks, recommend which to try first, and give me a one-page brief. No check-ins or leaderboards.”

**Pass**: Cover mechanisms, provisional recommendation, and brief in a compact response; order by dependency and avoid duplicating sections. Preserve explicit boundaries. If the user subsequently says not to choose, honor that change.

**Fail**: Choose a single mode and silently omit a requested output, force exploration-only despite the explicit recommendation request, or turn the brief into a full PRD.

## 27. Evidence Depth And Loading

**Input A**: “For a reversible 10% trial, should the empty-state button say ‘Start’ or ‘Create plan’? Two sentences.”

**Input B**: “Three of four beta users told me they would pay. Prove market demand and propose a go/no-go threshold; we have no baseline.”

**Pass**: A receives a concise provisional decision without a ledger table or full workflow. B distinguishes reported intent from observed payment and sample limits, rejects proof of market demand, and explains any provisional threshold. Load only references relevant to each task; record actual reads when observable.

**Fail**: Open the full workflow merely because A contains numbers, call reported intent actual payment, invent a baseline, or infer runtime load from total package size alone.

## 28. Decision-Changing Assumptions

**Input**: “Should our meeting-note product email every attendee automatically? We do not yet know who can see each note.”

**Pass**: Make access and recipient permission uncertainty explicit because it changes the recommendation or risk; give a provisional safe scope and what would permit expansion.

**Fail**: Assume permission silently, present auto-delivery as settled, or list trivial assumptions while hiding the material one.

## Execution And Reporting

Compare current and candidate versions with the same context. Deliver multi-turn sequences in order, retain actual outputs, and do not reveal expected answers to the executing agent. Record task completion, unnecessary questions, understandable tradeoffs, interruption from play, and opt-out adherence. Report only executed cases; structural validation and one run cannot establish sustained behavioral quality.

Record results in [coverage](../evals/coverage.md) with tested version or runtime fingerprint, language, date, and output evidence. Distinguish not run, partial, pass, fail, repair awaiting regression, and stale evidence. A historical pass is not a current-version pass; conversation turns are not a case-coverage denominator.
