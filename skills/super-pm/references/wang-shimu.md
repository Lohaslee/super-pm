## Distilled Principles

### WS-P01 Understand the market structure before ideating

- Source basis: WS-S01, WS-S05
- Distilled principle: A product opportunity is shaped by market participants, user movement, substitutes, supply, distribution, and changing constraints, not only by unmet feature requests.
- Use when: Starting a 0-to-1 concept or entering a crowded category.
- Ask: How does value currently move, where are users dissatisfied, and what structural change makes a new position possible?
- Decision rule: Define a differentiated strategic position before selecting feature patterns.
- Guardrail: Market maps are hypotheses and must be checked against direct user and participant evidence.
- Confidence: high

### WS-P02 Find the breakthrough mechanism

- Source basis: WS-S01, WS-S04, WS-S05
- Distilled principle: A breakthrough product usually has a small number of mutually reinforcing mechanisms that make the experience categorically better, not a longer feature list.
- Use when: A proposal is mostly parity plus minor improvements.
- Ask: What mechanism changes user behavior or value creation enough that competitors cannot match it by adding one screen?
- Decision rule: Protect and test the distinctive mechanism before expanding breadth.
- Guardrail: Difference alone is not value; the mechanism must solve a meaningful user problem.
- Confidence: high

### WS-P03 Study product evolution, not only the current interface

- Source basis: WS-S03, WS-S05
- Distilled principle: A product's present form reflects accumulated constraints, experiments, users, and stages; copying it without that history copies conclusions without causes.
- Use when: Benchmarking a mature competitor.
- Ask: Which problem did this feature solve when introduced, and do we have the same stage and constraints?
- Decision rule: Benchmark the decision path and mechanism, then design for the current stage of the new product.
- Guardrail: Historical explanation should not make legacy choices immune to challenge.
- Confidence: high

### WS-P04 Segment by behavior and situation

- Source basis: WS-S02, WS-S03, WS-S05
- Distilled principle: Actionable user groups are distinguished by goals, contexts, behavior, and constraints more than broad demographic labels.
- Use when: Defining recommendation, content, activity, or niche scenarios.
- Ask: Which repeated behavior signals a distinct need and a product response worth supporting?
- Decision rule: Create a segment only when it predicts different product decisions or value mechanisms.
- Guardrail: Avoid micro-segments that cannot support meaningful research or delivery.
- Confidence: high

### WS-P05 Combine qualitative insight with behavioral data

- Source basis: WS-S02, WS-S04, WS-S05
- Distilled principle: Interviews reveal motives and language; data reveals patterns and scale; product judgment connects them without treating either as sufficient alone.
- Use when: Teams argue intuition versus analytics.
- Ask: What behavior needs explanation, and which user conversation can test that explanation?
- Decision rule: Use data to locate and size a pattern, then qualitative evidence to understand and redesign it.
- Guardrail: Correlation and memorable stories both require competing explanations.
- Confidence: high

### WS-P06 Design the cold start as a complete product

- Source basis: WS-S01, WS-S04, WS-S05
- Distilled principle: Content and community products must provide enough initial value before network effects exist and deliberately seed the behaviors the mature system needs.
- Use when: A product relies on creators, comments, recommendations, or social proof.
- Ask: What valuable experience exists for the first user, and who creates the first credible supply?
- Decision rule: Define manual, editorial, or focused seeding as an explicit temporary mechanism with a transition condition.
- Guardrail: Artificial activity must not deceive users about who or what is real.
- Confidence: high

### WS-P07 Make content serve a user scenario

- Source basis: WS-S02, WS-S03
- Distilled principle: Content selection and recommendation should begin from the user's situation and desired outcome rather than the platform's inventory categories.
- Use when: Building feeds, playlists, knowledge products, or media experiences.
- Ask: In what moment is the user choosing, and what job should the content perform then?
- Decision rule: Organize discovery around recognizable user intent when it predicts better outcomes than taxonomy alone.
- Guardrail: Context inference needs user control and should not become invasive surveillance.
- Confidence: high

### WS-P08 Treat product decisions as an explicit chain

- Source basis: WS-S05
- Distilled principle: Strong product work links market judgment, user insight, product objective, mechanism, scope, and evaluation so later teams can understand why a choice exists.
- Use when: Roadmaps contain decisions with lost rationale.
- Ask: Which upstream judgment makes this requirement necessary, and what evidence would reverse it?
- Decision rule: Record major assumptions and revisit triggers with the decision, not in a separate retrospective.
- Guardrail: Documentation should support decisions, not become a substitute for making them.
- Confidence: high

### WS-P09 Match organization to the product challenge

- Source basis: WS-S01, WS-S05, WS-S06
- Distilled principle: Discovery, scaling, content operations, and technical platform work require different capability mixes and decision rhythms.
- Use when: A team structure persists despite a changed product stage.
- Ask: What is the current bottleneck, and which capabilities must collaborate closely to remove it?
- Decision rule: Organize a small accountable group around the current product risk, then adjust as the risk changes.
- Guardrail: Reorganization creates cost; change structure only when ownership or capability mismatch is material.
- Confidence: medium

### WS-P10 Let constraints sharpen creativity

- Source basis: WS-S02, WS-S04, WS-S05
- Distilled principle: Limited budget, traffic, content, or engineering capacity can force a clearer mechanism and more focused user entry point.
- Use when: A team responds to constraints by making an undifferentiated smaller copy.
- Ask: Which constraint forces us to choose the highest-leverage user and mechanism?
- Decision rule: Preserve the distinctive hypothesis while reducing audience, breadth, or automation around it.
- Guardrail: Some constraints make the promise impossible; name that rather than romanticizing scarcity.
- Confidence: medium
